About

I grew up taking things apart to see how they worked, and studying computer engineering in Pune gave that curiosity a direction. The projects I enjoyed most went under the hood: adding schedulers to the xv6 kernel, writing a shell in C, and building an HTTP server by hand to learn what a browser actually sends. By final year I had published an IEEE paper as first author on patient-controlled health records.

My first job, at Rakuten Symphony, put that curiosity to work on two systems: an S3-compatible object store written in C, where good engineering shows up as data that is simply always there, and MDCAP, a Kubernetes automation platform that provisions and upgrades fleets of servers and clusters. Two years of garbage collectors, erasure coding, workflow engines, Helm deployments, and failure testing gave me a habit I still rely on: design for the hard case first, and the easy cases take care of themselves.

In 2025 I moved to Abu Dhabi to work where models meet the real world. I spend my days with HSE managers who want their people safer, engineers who build the platform, and field crews on drilling rigs and offshore sites. The best moment is standing on a site when the first real alert fires on a camera I helped bring up. That is when a model stops being a metric and starts protecting someone.

I enjoy explaining this work as much as doing it. I have demoed the platform live at GITEX and ADIPEC to everyone from field engineers to executives, and I am writing up what I have learned about edge AI, distributed storage, and running models on Kubernetes. The work I enjoy most sits where the model, the infrastructure, and the people relying on it all have to come together.
